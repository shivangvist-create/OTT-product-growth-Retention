# Retention Analysis

## Why I Looked at Retention

For an OTT app, getting new users is important, but it is also important to see if users continue using the app.

So, in this part of the case study, I looked at how many users come back after using the app for the first time.

The data used here is simulated.

## Retention Data

| User Group | Users | Day 1 | Day 7 | Day 30 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| New Users | 5,000 | 62% | 38% | 24% |
| Trial Users | 3,000 | 68% | 45% | 30% |
| Paid Users | 2,000 | 78% | 58% | 42% |

## What I Observed

From this data, I noticed that:

- Retention decreases over time.
- New users have lower retention than paid users.
- Trial users have better retention than new users.
- Paid users have the highest retention in this example.

This made me think that helping new users find useful content early could improve retention.

## Possible Problem

A new user may open the app but may not find something interesting to watch.

If this happens repeatedly, the user may stop coming back to the app.

## Product Idea

One idea I considered is improving personalized recommendations.

The app could use things like:

- Favourite genres
- Selected interests
- Watch history
- Previous content watched

to show more relevant content to users.

## Metrics I Would Track

If this feature is tested, I would look at:

- Day 7 Retention
- Day 30 Retention
- Average Watch Time
- Number of Sessions
- Content Completion Rate

## What I Learned

This analysis helped me understand how retention data can be used to identify a product problem and think about a possible solution.

## Note

The numbers used in this analysis are simulated for this case study and are not from a real OTT company.
